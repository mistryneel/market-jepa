## Key findings for the reviewer

1. **The primary endpoint cannot be computed with a daily or 5-minute realized covariance.**
   - **Why.** Five daily return vectors of about 500 stocks give a realized covariance Σ* of rank at most 5. Its log-determinant is −∞, so the Stein loss is +∞ for every positive-definite forecast. Five-minute data gives at most 390 returns over 5 days, still fewer than 500 stocks. Sampling every minute could reach full rank in principle, but for 500 stocks the matrix would be ill-conditioned and distorted by asynchronous trading and microstructure noise.
   - **Fix that keeps the intent.** Score the forecast-dependent part of the loss, the Gaussian negative log-likelihood (multivariate QLIKE), (1/n)[log det Σ̂ + tr(Σ̂⁻¹Σ*)]. Wherever Stein is finite, paired model differences equal Stein differences divided by n.
   - **Why the fix is sound.** For a fixed forecast the loss is affine in Σ*. Suppose Σ* is conditionally unbiased for the forecast's own target (same horizon and scale) and the forecast uses only information available at the origin. Then expected-loss rankings match those under the true covariance, whatever the rank of Σ*. This last step is our inference: [Laurent+ 2013](#r-laurent-2013) prove robustness only for positive-definite proxies.
   - **It needs Σ̂ to be positive definite, and at n ≈ 500 that binds.** Naive, short-window and EWMA forecasts are singular or nearly so. Pre-specify one positive-definite repair for every model.
   - Sources: [Laurent+ 2013](#r-laurent-2013), [Patton & Sheppard 2009](#r-patton-sheppard-2009), [Bollerslev+ 2018](#r-bollerslev-2018), [Gneiting & Raftery 2007](#r-gneiting-raftery-2007), [S4 CD-DFM](#s4). Details in [section 3](#3-primary-endpoint-first-read).
2. **Event timing must be pre-specified: the effect may sit on day 0 or on day +1.**
   - CPI is released at 08:30 ET, before the open. FOMC statements come out at 14:00 ET. [BLS CPI schedule](#r-bls-cpi-schedule) [USMPD](#r-usmpd)
   - In interest-rate futures, covariance jumps at announcements are identified within about 90 minutes of the release. [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15) With daily data that would put much of the effect on day 0, which is our inference.
   - VaR violations around FOMC cluster on the day after, so day +1 must stay in the window. [S14 Bui+](#s14)†
   - Day −1 is not a clean baseline before FOMC: stocks drift up and volatility is unusually low before scheduled announcements. [Lucca & Moench 2015](#r-lucca-moench-2015) [Bomfim 2003](#r-bomfim-2003)†
3. **Power is a real risk.**
   - The test window (1 Jan 2022 to 30 Jun 2026) holds 36 scheduled FOMC decisions and 53 CPI releases. [FOMC calendar](#r-fomc-calendar) [BLS CPI schedule](#r-bls-cpi-schedule)
   - 19 CPI–FOMC pairs fall within 7 calendar days of each other. In 10 of them the day 1–5 windows share trading days (all five on 12 Jun 2024). In the other 9 the earlier window ends on the later announcement day.
   - The documented daily volatility uplift on announcement days is modest: about 4% for market realized volatility [Savor & Wilson 2013](#r-savor-wilson-2013), and equity returns about 10% more volatile on FOMC days [Bernanke & Kuttner 2005](#r-bernanke-kuttner-2005).
   - Shared event dates break the independence that standard event aggregation assumes. [MacKinlay 1997](#r-mackinlay-1997)
   - We need a minimum-detectable-effect analysis on pre-2022 per-event loss differences. In [S2 Mahns+](#s2) an event-blind JEPA latent barely reacted to scheduled FOMC/CPI days. That motivates explicit event inputs, but it is not a power input.
4. **H2's contrast must be pinned down, because its test depends on it.** The brief allows three readings:
   - **(a) With vs without event inputs.** These models are nested. Use Giacomini–White on fixed-length rolling refits; it does not cover expanding windows. [Giacomini & White 2006](#r-giacomini-white-2006)
   - **(b) Pretrained plus events vs an equal-size supervised model with the same events.** These are non-nested, so DM or the paired bootstrap applies.
   - **(c) A 2×2 interaction** of the two.
   - Clark–West is derived for squared-error forecasts of a scalar and has no analogue for the Stein/NLL loss. [Clark & West 2007](#r-clark-west-2007)
   - With parameters estimated once on a fixed window, DM/GW statistics can diverge under the null. [McCracken 2020](#r-mccracken-2020) Our reading: a model trained once and frozen supports only a comparison of the two fitted forecasters.
5. **Realized GMV variance deserves co-primary status for H1.**
   - It stays defined when Σ* is singular.
   - Ledoit and Wolf argue the minimum-variance loss is the relevant one for portfolio use. [Ledoit & Wolf 2017](#r-ledoit-wolf-2017) [Ledoit & Wolf 2022](#r-ledoit-wolf-2022)
   - Three peer-reviewed 2026 seeds evaluate with it. [S11 Reis+](#s11) [S12 Bongiorno+](#s12) [S13 Yang+](#s13)
   - Matrix losses and portfolio metrics ranked models differently in [S4 CD-DFM](#s4) and [S11 Reis+](#s11), and [S12 Bongiorno+](#s12) argues that a Frobenius optimum is not a GMV optimum.
   - **Caveats.** It ignores the forecast's overall scale, so pair it with the calibration ratio. It grows with event volatility, so pool per-event log ratios against a baseline.
6. **Several brief citations are wrong.**
   - Six seed titles differ from the brief, five of them substantively.
   - One venue is wrong: [S14 Bui+](#s14)† is in the *International* Review of Economics & Finance.
   - "Covariance jumps at macroeconomic announcements" is really [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15), Li, Todorov & Zhang (2024, REStat). It tests the rank of policy shocks in interest-rate futures.
   - Only 8 of the 17 seed links are peer-reviewed. See [section 1](#1-seed-sources-what-we-could-open).
7. **We found no public benchmark for multivariate covariance forecasting.**
   - 18 searches found nothing with a covariance task, public data, a fixed protocol and a leaderboard. The searches are not exhaustive.
   - The closest items are VOLARE (a data archive), M6 (return ranking) and Kaggle Optiver (univariate 10-minute volatility). This is a gap our paper could fill.
   - "FinTSB" does exist (Frontiers of Computer Science, 2026), but it is return ranking on China A-shares. [FinTSB](#r-fintsb)
8. **Leakage hazards specific to us:**
   - Kronos was pretrained on data through June 2024, including US equities and crypto. That overlaps our test window and makes its crypto results in-distribution. [S6 Kronos](#s6)
   - Any LLM event encoder whose training data extend past 1 Jan 2022 risks memorized narratives. [S8 Profit Mirage](#s8) [Glasserman & Lin 2024](#r-glasserman-lin-2024)
   - In [S10 Gençay](#s10) a planted look-ahead oracle passed the deflated Sharpe ratio, so statistics alone do not catch leakage.
9. **Event data:**
   - The SF Fed USMPD (to 28 Oct 2026) and the Jarociński–Karadi series (to Sep 2026) cover our window. The Bauer–Swanson file stops in Dec 2023. All three are FOMC-only. [USMPD](#r-usmpd) [JK shocks](#r-jk-shocks) [Bauer–Swanson data](#r-bauerswanson-data)
   - No free CPI consensus series was found. [Cleveland Fed nowcast](#r-cleveland-fed-nowcast)
   - The October 2025 CPI, due 13 Nov 2025, was never published, so no CPI came out in November 2025. [BLS 2025 lapse](#r-bls-2025-lapse)
10. **Gaps in the brief's design that reviewers will find:**
    - Earnings are a model input but no evaluation handles them, even though betas move for 2–5 days after earnings. [Patton & Verardo 2012](#r-patton-verardo-2012)†
    - Event-input timestamps are not tied to the forecast origin.
    - H1 ("matches") needs a non-inferiority test, not just failure to reject.
    - Baselines need the same tuning budget as our model.

#### A4. Reference baselines (proposed new category `baseline`)

Every forecast scored by the Stein/NLL loss must be positive definite. One pre-specified repair, such as shrinkage to a diagonal target or an eigenvalue floor, applies to all models. A non-PD forecast counts as a failure, never as a silent fallback.

## 3. Primary endpoint: first read

**Verdict: keep a Gaussian-likelihood (Stein-family) loss as the primary endpoint, but redefine it so it can be computed.** Six further choices also need to be fixed before the test set is unlocked.

**3.1 Loss.** With rank(Σ*) ≤ 5 ≪ n, the brief's

$$\mathrm{Stein}(\hat\Sigma,\Sigma^*)=\mathrm{tr}(\Sigma^*\hat\Sigma^{-1})-\log\det(\Sigma^*\hat\Sigma^{-1})-n$$

is $+\infty$ for every positive-definite forecast. The same quantity splits as

$$\mathrm{Stein}=\underbrace{\big[\log\det\hat\Sigma+\mathrm{tr}(\hat\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma^*)\big]}_{\text{Gaussian NLL / multivariate QLIKE}}-\big[\log\det\Sigma^*+n\big].$$

Score the first bracket per asset, $L=\tfrac1n[\log\det\hat\Sigma+\mathrm{tr}(\hat\Sigma^{-1}\Sigma^*)]$. Take $\Sigma^*=\tfrac15\sum_{d=1}^{5}r_{t+d}r_{t+d}^{\top}$ (uncentred) or the 5-day sum, and put the forecast on the same scale.

- **What it needs.** $L$ needs only $\hat\Sigma$ to be positive definite. At n ≈ 500 that rules out raw naive and EWMA forecasts, so pre-specify one PD repair for all models. The loss weights each eigen-direction of $\hat\Sigma$ by the inverse eigenvalue, so the repair largely sets those baselines' scores.
- **Equivalence to Stein.** Wherever Stein is finite, paired model differences equal Stein differences, divided by n in the per-asset form.
- **Its pedigree.**
  - It is the forecast-dependent part of the $b=-2$ member of Patton & Sheppard's multivariate robust class. That member is the normalized Stein form, whose proxy-only terms can be dropped. [Patton & Sheppard 2009](#r-patton-sheppard-2009)
  - It is one of the two losses [Bollerslev+ 2018](#r-bollerslev-2018) use for realized-covariance forecasts (with a positive-definite proxy, N = 10).
  - It is the negated Dawid–Sebastiani score of [Gneiting & Raftery 2007](#r-gneiting-raftery-2007), averaged over the daily return vectors.
  - [S4 CD-DFM](#s4) trains on it, and [Johansson+ 2023](#r-johansson-2023) evaluate covariance predictors by average Gaussian log-likelihood.
- **Robustness, with its conditions.** For a fixed $\hat\Sigma\succ0$ the loss is affine in Σ*. Suppose $\hat\Sigma$ uses only information at the origin, and E[Σ*|origin] equals the forecast's target: same 5-day horizon and scale, uncentred second moments, overnight returns included. Then expected-loss rankings match those under the true conditional covariance, whatever the rank of Σ*. Single-sample rankings still carry proxy noise.
  - [Laurent+ 2013](#r-laurent-2013) prove this only for positive-definite proxies, so the singular case is our inference and should be stated as such.
- **Orientation matters for the result, not just the name.** [Ledoit & Wolf 2018](#r-ledoit-wolf-2018) and [Ledoit & Wolf 2022](#r-ledoit-wolf-2022) define Stein's loss with the *true* matrix inverted, and call the brief's orientation "inverse Stein". The target-inverted form needs Σ*⁻¹ and is non-linear in the proxy, so it is not ranking-robust. Under a noisy unbiased proxy it rewards understating risk. [Ledoit & Wolf 2022](#r-ledoit-wolf-2022) warn against losses that invert the population covariance when p > n. Cite [Laurent+ 2013](#r-laurent-2013) and [Patton & Sheppard 2009](#r-patton-sheppard-2009) for the brief's form, not only James–Stein.

**3.2 Timing, origin and inputs.**
- Pre-specify the forecast origin: the close of day −1, or the close of day 0 after the release.
- Report day −1, day 0, each of days 1–5, and the cumulative loss. CPI (08:30 ET) falls before the open and FOMC (14:00 ET) during trading, so "day 1" means different things for the two event types. [BLS CPI schedule](#r-bls-cpi-schedule) [USMPD](#r-usmpd) [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15) [S14 Bui+](#s14)†
- The origin fixes which event inputs are legal.
  - **Close of day −1:** inputs are limited to ex-ante information (calendar, event type, consensus, implied vol). Realized surprises can only stratify results.
  - **Close of day 0:** surprises may be inputs, but day 0 leaves the forecast window. [USMPD](#r-usmpd) [Acosta+ 2025](#r-acosta-2025)

**3.3 Pooling and stratification.**
- Report FOMC and CPI separately as well as pooled.
- Stratify FOMC events by surprise sign and size using USMPD or Jarociński–Karadi surprises, which are FOMC-only.
- Stratify CPI events by the size of the realized index move until a consensus source is chosen.
- Treat overlapping FOMC/CPI windows and each stock's own earnings windows explicitly. [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15) [S14 Bui+](#s14)† [MacKinlay 1997](#r-mackinlay-1997) [Patton & Verardo 2012](#r-patton-verardo-2012)†

**3.4 Identification of H2.**
- Fix the contrast first (Key finding 4). The with-vs-without-event-inputs contrast is nested: use Giacomini–White on fixed-length rolling refits, unconditional or conditional on event indicators. [Giacomini & White 2006](#r-giacomini-white-2006) [McCracken 2020](#r-mccracken-2020)
- Define the H2 effect as the post-event gain minus the gain on matched non-event days.
- Add an untrained-encoder control [S1 Market-1T](#s1) [S17 causal-jepa](#s17) and a matched non-event placebo window [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15). An event-input permutation placebo is our own proposal.

**3.5 Power.**
- Run a minimum-detectable-effect analysis on pre-2022 events before unlocking the test set.
- Consider adding pre-2022 events in a walk-forward design. A single 2022–mid-2026 window is the kind of narrow slice [S1 Market-1T](#s1) warns about, and it may be dominated by the 2022 tightening cycle.
- Event counts from official calendars: **36 scheduled FOMC decisions and 53 CPI releases** fall between 1 Jan 2022 and 30 Jun 2026. There were no unscheduled rate decisions; the one other FOMC action was a notation vote on 22 Aug 2025 on the longer-run strategy statement. **19 CPI–FOMC pairs** fall within 7 calendar days of each other. In 10 of them the day 1–5 windows share trading days (all five on 2024-06-12, when CPI came at 08:30 ET and FOMC at 14:00 ET). In the other 9, exactly 7 days apart, the earlier window ends on the later announcement day; exact overlap depends on the exchange holiday calendar. The October 2025 CPI, due 13 Nov 2025, was never published, so no CPI came out in November 2025. The September 2025 CPI moved to 24 Oct and the November CPI to 18 Dec 2025. A separate 2026 lapse moved the January 2026 CPI to 13 Feb 2026. From 1 Jul to 12 Nov 2026 there were 3 more FOMC decisions and 5 more CPI releases. [FOMC calendar](#r-fomc-calendar) [BLS CPI schedule](#r-bls-cpi-schedule) [BLS 2025 lapse](#r-bls-2025-lapse)

**3.6 Co-primary for H1.** Make realized GMV variance co-primary (or the key secondary) for H1, and pre-specify:
- unconstrained and long-only weights [Jagannathan & Ma 2003](#r-jagannathan-ma-2003)†;
- portfolio formation at the forecast origin;
- the realized definition (w′Σ*w);
- pooling of per-event log ratios against a baseline, since the metric grows with event volatility;
- the test ([Ledoit & Wolf 2011](#r-ledoit-wolf-2011) or the paired bootstrap);
- Holm across co-primaries.

**3.7 What stays as proposed.**
- The universe is point-in-time and rebuilt monthly from prior-month data, as in [S1 Market-1T](#s1). [S1 Market-1T](#s1) itself uses thresholds (price > $5, dollar volume ≥ $20M, trading activity; about 700 names) rather than a top-500 rank. Names that later delist must stay in.
- Pairing within period is supported by [S1 Market-1T](#s1) (per-month paired differences) and [S10 Gençay](#s10) (a paired block bootstrap). The month-block bootstrap itself is our choice, grounded in [Künsch 1989](#r-kunsch-1989)† and [Politis & Romano 1994](#r-politis-romano-1994)†.
- Secondary checks: the calibration ratio, bias statistics and rank IC.

## 4. Where the literature disagrees (so far)

- **Matrix losses vs portfolio losses.**
  - [Ledoit & Wolf 2017](#r-ledoit-wolf-2017), [Ledoit & Wolf 2020](#r-ledoit-wolf-2020) and [Ledoit & Wolf 2022](#r-ledoit-wolf-2022) favour the minimum-variance loss, but show that the MV-optimal nonlinear shrinkage coincides with the Frobenius-optimal one.
  - [S12 Bongiorno+](#s12) disputes that equivalence in practice and trains directly on 5-day GMV variance.
  - [S11 Reis+](#s11) uses only Frobenius/Euclidean as matrix losses, plus long-only GMV variance; its Frobenius winner was not the lowest-variance model.
  - Rankings change across metrics within [S4 CD-DFM](#s4): Ledoit–Wolf has by far the worst Stein loss (521 vs 302–322) but GMV calibration (0.92) as good as the proposed model's (0.93).
- **Stein orientation.** Statistics papers invert the true matrix, econometrics papers the forecast, and only the latter is ranking-robust. See section 3.1.
- **Bures–Wasserstein:** [S4 CD-DFM](#s4)'s paper prints the distance, but its code returns the squared distance.
- **Pooled vs event-by-event analysis.** [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15) argues that pooled event studies rest on a fragile homogeneity assumption. [S2 Mahns+](#s2) and [S14 Bui+](#s14)† pool by event category, and our endpoint pools FOMC and CPI.
- **Do scheduled events move second moments?**
  - [S15 Li+ (REStat)](#s15) finds covariance jumps at FOMC (interest-rate futures, intraday).
  - [S14 Bui+](#s14)† finds VaR failures around FOMC (abstract only).
  - [S2 Mahns+](#s2) finds no significant latent displacement on FOMC/CPI days.
  - The daily volatility uplift is about 4% in [Savor & Wilson 2013](#r-savor-wilson-2013) and about 10% in [Bernanke & Kuttner 2005](#r-bernanke-kuttner-2005).
- **Kronos's claims vs its own tables.** [S6 Kronos](#s6) claims consistent state of the art, but GARCH beats every Kronos size on NASDAQ volatility.
- **Effective rank as a health signal.**
  - [S16 jepa-trader](#s16) reads RankMe 65–88 as "no collapse".
  - [S17 causal-jepa](#s17) says rank must not be read as a health signal, because it rose while utility fell.
  - In [S1 Market-1T](#s1) supervised models have the lowest RankMe but forecast best.
  - [Garrido+ 2023](#r-garrido-2023) themselves restrict RankMe to within-method comparisons.
- **Purged CV vs a single split.**
  - [S4 CD-DFM](#s4), [S2 Mahns+](#s2) and [S3 STOCK-JEPA](#s3) purge or embargo.
  - [S10 Gençay](#s10) uses one design/evaluation split with a 1-day lag.
  - [Bergmeir+ 2018](#r-bergmeir-2018)† show that standard K-fold CV is valid for purely autoregressive models whose errors are uncorrelated.
- **One-day vs intraday policy surprises.**
  - [Hanson & Stein 2015](#r-hanson-stein-2015) use 1–2-day changes in the 2-year yield.
  - [Nakamura & Steinsson 2018](#r-nakamura-steinsson-2018) show one-day windows are confounded by background noise.
- **Does JEPA-style pretraining help?**
  - Yes in [S3 STOCK-JEPA](#s3), though with 3 runs, no significance tests, and much of its edge over JEPA baselines coming from readout and input design.
  - The sign flips by backbone in [S7 Feng+](#s7).
  - In [S1 Market-1T](#s1) many SSL methods fall below a random encoder.
  - In [S2 Mahns+](#s2) the latent encodes risk geometry but adds little.
  - No trading edge in [S16 jepa-trader](#s16); no gain over raw features in [S17 causal-jepa](#s17).
- **Do pretrained models' look-ahead exposures inflate accuracy?**
  - [Meyer+ 2025](#r-meyer-2025) document overlap and inflated scores.
  - A September 2026 preprint (abstract only) finds post-origin vintages *reduced* US forecast accuracy. [Chen+ 2026](#r-chen-2026)†
- **IC vs Sharpe as the return metric.**
  - [S1 Market-1T](#s1) prefers IC because harness choices move Sharpe more than encoders do.
  - [S3 STOCK-JEPA](#s3) headlines RankIC and also reports net Sharpe.
  - [S5 Rahimikia+](#s5) reports R²_OOS and net Sharpe side by side, and they disagree.
- **Calibration and realized-covariance conventions.**
  - [S4 CD-DFM](#s4)'s paper uses a variance ratio with unconstrained GMV; its code uses a median volatility ratio with long-only GMV.
  - Realized covariance is uncentred 1/Δ in [S4 CD-DFM](#s4) and demeaned 1/(F−1) in [S11 Reis+](#s11).

## 5. Open questions for review

1. **H2's contrast and estimand.** Which contrast does H2 test?
   - (a) Pretrained model with vs without event inputs (nested).
   - (b) Pretrained plus events vs an equal-size supervised model with the same events (non-nested).
   - (c) A 2×2 design, where H2 is the difference in event-input gains.

   Also: is the endpoint the raw post-event paired NLL difference, or its difference from matched non-event days?
2. **The deciding test.** Which single pre-registered test, sidedness and α decide the primary endpoint? For example: a one-sided month-cluster bootstrap of the paired per-asset NLL difference at 5%, with DM-HLN and GW as sensitivity analyses.
   - Is "better than strong baselines" decided by all pairwise tests (intersection–union), by SPA, or by MCS?
3. **The loss.** Adopt the Gaussian-NLL (multivariate QLIKE) form as the computable primary loss, keeping the "Stein" name with a footnote? Which positive-definite repair applies to all forecasts?
4. **Forecast origin.** Close of day −1 (event inputs ex ante only) or close of day 0 (surprises allowed, day 0 out of the window)? Include day 0 in the endpoint, or report it separately?
5. **Endpoints for H1, H3 and H4.**
   - H1: make realized GMV variance co-primary? What is the non-inferiority margin? Which days count as "ordinary" (excluding FOMC/CPI days −1 to +5 and each stock's earnings windows)?
   - H3: which crypto universe (point-in-time top-N Binance pairs, delisted ones included), target and period (DVOL only from Mar 2021)? Is crypto excluded from all pretraining?
   - H4: which outcome (announcement-time jumps or daily RV jumps) and which test?
6. **Earnings.** Which earnings data source with before-open and after-close timestamps? Report H2 with and without earnings inputs?
7. **Test window.** Keep 2022 to mid-2026 only, or add pre-2022 events in a walk-forward design for power?
8. **LLMs.** Will event inputs pass through any LLM or text encoder? This decides how much of section F applies.
9. **Foundation-model baselines.** Which ones (Kronos, Chronos, TimesFM) are in scope, and is it acceptable to score them only on events after their pretraining cutoffs?
10. **Data.**
    - CRSP (CIZ format; check its release lag for 2026) or Massive/Polygon files for 2022–2026?
    - Do we have intraday data for a realized-covariance target?
    - Should we license CPI consensus data?
11. **Public release.**
    - Make a public event-window covariance benchmark an explicit contribution?
    - What can be redistributed? CRSP returns and VIX cannot be shipped, so release code, dates and identifiers only. Binance-derived targets must stay CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Market-1T-derived features need licence clarification.
12. **Schema extensions.** Approve:
    - categories `baseline` and `dataset`;
    - the status values `our design` and `gap`;
    - per-source `accessed`, `source_type`, `read_depth` and `version_read` fields;
    - a `computable_form` field for losses such as Stein.

## 6. Method and limits of this pass

- **Four passes with research agents.** Eight agents read the 17 seed links in full where possible. Nine opened the canonical originals and benchmark pages the brief names. Three opened the further references the seeds surfaced and counted events from the official FOMC and BLS calendars. Five then reviewed this document adversarially (three claim audits, a completeness critic and an endpoint-math check); their findings were applied.
- **Read depth and access problems.**
  - Paywalled publisher pages (Wiley, ScienceDirect, SSRN, ACM, IEEE, now publishers) often returned HTTP 403.
  - For [S11 Reis+](#s11), [S12 Bongiorno+](#s12) and [S13 Yang+](#s13) we read arXiv versions; their published versions may differ. The source list notes other cases where the version read differs from the version linked.
  - [S14 Bui+](#s14)† was read at abstract and reference-list level only, although it is open access.
  - OpenReview pages showed a browser check, so no reviews were read.
  - Kaggle pages render client-side, so competition metrics come from secondary write-ups and should be confirmed on Kaggle.
  - For benchmarks found during the gap search, the agents recorded what each one covers but not how deeply they read it; the source list says "not recorded" there.
  - Some agents saved PDFs through the fetch tool and extracted their text locally in the session scratchpad, outside the repository. Nothing was downloaded with shell tools.
- **Prompt injection, ignored.** One verification agent reported that several OpenAlex API responses asked automated clients to register an API key with the user's email address. It did not act on them and sent no personal data. Two other agents reported no such requests, and the remaining agents' outputs do not mention any.
- **Statuses are provisional.** "Widely used" will be confirmed in the full pass with at least two credible 2023–2026 papers per entry, and every must-have will get two sources or its canonical original.
